CUPED and Experiment Variance Reduction

Learning goals

  • Derive the optimal linear adjustment and its variance reduction.
  • Explain why a pre-treatment adjustment preserves an experiment’s estimand in expectation.
  • Interpret small experimental lifts without confusing raw and adjusted inference.

Intuition

Customers differ in baseline activity. Last month’s activity predicts some of this month’s activity even without treatment. Subtracting that predictable variation can make the difference between randomized groups more precise.

CUPED uses pre-experiment covariates to improve precision. Randomization supplies the identification argument; the adjustment does not turn a selected observational comparison into a randomized experiment.

Worked example

Adjust two users

Suppose \(E[X]=10\), \(\operatorname{Var}(X)=25\), and \(\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X)=15\). Then \(\theta^*=15/25=0.6\).

A user with \(Y=18,X=15\) has adjusted outcome \(18-0.6(15-10)=15\). Another with \(Y=12,X=5\) has adjusted outcome \(12-0.6(5-10)=15\). Their different baseline activity explained the apparent outcome difference.

Precision in correlation units

If \(\rho=0.7\), residual variance is \(1-0.7^2=0.51\) of original variance: 49% reduction, not 51% reduction. Under comparable variance and design conditions, the same standard error would otherwise require about \(1/0.51=1.96\) times as many observations.

Correlation Variance retained Variance removed Approximate equivalent sample multiplier
0.5 0.75 25% 1.33
0.7 0.51 49% 1.96
0.9 0.19 81% 5.26

Read a small lift carefully

In a synthetic teaching example, suppose the control mean is 9.000 and the treatment mean is 9.007. Their absolute difference is 0.007 and relative difference is

\[9.007/9.000-1\approx0.000778=0.0778\%.\]

The example illustrates that very large experiments can detect small effects. Those means alone do not reproduce a CUPED standard error or adjusted p-value: one needs the relevant adjusted variance, covariance, counts, and platform estimand. A raw two-sample calculation and an adjusted platform test need not agree. A significant visit metric also does not imply significant orders, payment amounts, or every subgroup.

Theory and assumptions

Derive the adjustment

Set

\[Y^*=Y-\theta(X-E[X]).\]

Since subtracting a constant does not change variance,

\[\operatorname{Var}(Y^*)=\operatorname{Var}(Y)+\theta^2\operatorname{Var}(X)-2\theta\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X).\]

Differentiate with respect to \(\theta\) and set the derivative to zero:

\[2\theta\operatorname{Var}(X)-2\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X)=0, \qquad\theta^*=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}.\]

For \(\operatorname{Var}(X)>0\), the second derivative is positive. Substitution gives

\[\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(Y^*) &=\operatorname{Var}(Y)-\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X)^2}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}\\ &=\operatorname{Var}(Y)(1-\rho_{YX}^2). \end{aligned}\]

This is a population linear-projection result. Estimated coefficients, arm heteroskedasticity, and finite-sample dependence affect the precise gain for an experimental difference estimator.

Why the effect is preserved

For a fixed adjustment coefficient,

\[\begin{aligned} E[Y^*\mid T=1]-E[Y^*\mid T=0] &=E[Y\mid T=1]-E[Y\mid T=0]\\ &\quad-\theta\{E[X\mid T=1]-E[X\mid T=0]\}. \end{aligned}\]

Randomization and a pre-treatment \(X\) make the final population contrast zero. The observed adjusted difference need not equal the raw sample difference because realized baseline imbalance is precisely what is being adjusted. Estimating \(\theta\) from the same small sample adds finite-sample considerations.

With several covariates, the linear projection coefficient is \(\Sigma_{XX}^{-1}\Sigma_{XY}\) when nonsingular. More flexible baseline predictions can remove nonlinear predictable variation, but require suitable training/cross-fitting and valid inference to avoid overfitting-induced bias.

Relationship to regression and ANCOVA

The one-variable \(\theta^*\) is the population OLS slope of \(Y\) on \(X\). Comparing residualized outcomes resembles covariate-adjusted regression \(Y\sim T+X\).

However, residualizing only \(Y\) using a pooled regression and then comparing treatment means is not generally algebraically identical in a finite sample to the treatment coefficient in ANCOVA. Frisch–Waugh–Lovell residualizes both \(Y\) and \(T\) with respect to \(X\). The methods align under appropriate randomization/asymptotic conditions, but implementation and uncertainty should be stated explicitly.

Practice and pitfalls

Use strongly prognostic pre-treatment variables, usually the same metric in a pre-period. Predefine that period and handle users with no history consistently; missing historical activity may itself be predictive. Do not condition on treatment-induced clicks, visits, or purchases.

Two reasons CUPED may not make a result significant are a genuinely small/zero effect and a weakly predictive baseline covariate. Limited sample size, noisy coefficient estimation, heterogeneous variance, or model misspecification can also limit gains.

Question CUPED DiD
Primary purpose Increase precision Construct an identifying comparison
Typical design Randomized experiment with baseline covariates Before/after treated and comparison groups
Key argument Treatment independent of baseline covariates Parallel untreated trends and related assumptions
Baseline coefficient Chosen for prediction/variance reduction Simple DiD subtracts baseline with coefficient 1

Predeclare the primary analysis; show raw and adjusted estimates transparently. Merely displaying both does not automatically double the hypothesis family, but selecting whichever p-value is significant does create a testing problem. Multiple outcomes or exploratory subgroups require an appropriate multiplicity strategy. Report per-randomization-unit lift, CIs, and guardrails rather than total count differences alone.

Interview questions

Why is \(\theta\) a regression slope? Minimizing adjusted outcome variance differentiates the same quadratic loss underlying a linear projection, giving covariance divided by variance.

Does CUPED change ATE? With a fixed pre-treatment adjustment in an RCT it preserves the population difference in expectation. The sample estimate can change because baseline imbalance is adjusted.

Is variance reduction \(1-\rho^2\)? That is the fraction retained. The fraction removed is \(\rho^2\).

Can I use machine learning? Yes, to predict baseline outcomes more flexibly with a design that controls overfitting and yields valid experimental inference. Better training fit alone is insufficient.

Summary

CUPED removes predictable baseline variation. Its value is narrower uncertainty around a properly identified experimental effect, with consistent reporting of adjusted inference and practical effect magnitude.
