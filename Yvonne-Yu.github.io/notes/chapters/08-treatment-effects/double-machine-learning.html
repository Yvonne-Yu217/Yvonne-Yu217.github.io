Double Machine Learning

Learning goals

  • Distinguish the structural outcome function from the conditional outcome regression.
  • Derive residual-on-residual estimation, orthogonality, and a sufficient nuisance-rate condition.
  • Design a complete observational analysis with valid timing, uncertainty, and experimental follow-up.

Intuition

Historical activity can predict both a user’s exposure and future return. DML uses flexible models to subtract the predictable baseline from both treatment and outcome, then estimates the relationship between what remains. Sample splitting prevents the same observation from training its own nuisance prediction.

This improves estimation under a causal identification argument. It does not make exposure random, remove unmeasured interest, or repair an outcome window that overlaps exposure measurement.

Worked example

Residuals by hand

Suppose held-out nuisance predictions produce the following residuals:

User Treatment residual \(\widetilde T\) Outcome residual \(\widetilde Y\) Product Treatment residual squared
A 0.5 0.12 0.060 0.25
B -0.5 -0.08 0.040 0.25
C 0.4 0.10 0.040 0.16
D -0.4 -0.06 0.024 0.16

Then

\[\hat\theta=\frac{0.164}{0.82}=0.20.\]

For a binary outcome and binary treatment under a constant-effect partially linear model, this is a 0.20 probability-unit effect, or 20 percentage points. This tiny example teaches the arithmetic; four records are not sufficient for credible high-dimensional causal inference.

A return-search study, from question to action

The source case asks which touchpoints after an anchor-day search predict later active return-search. A reusable design has these steps:

  1. Define a user-level eligible cohort at anchor day \(t_0\) and a precise active-return outcome, such as first qualifying return during \(t_0+2\) through \(t_0+7\).
  2. Predefine a treatment window that ends before the outcome window; do not end exposure counting at the outcome itself. That creates outcome-dependent opportunity to receive exposure.
  3. Group candidate actions: passive-search cards and query suggestions; search-result CTR/CVR, price visibility, product clicks and depth; video/livestream and marketplace exposure; experience and marketing measures. Decide which are treatments, mediators, or controls using the timeline rather than treating every available metric as a confounder.
  4. Freeze controls from the preceding 30 days: demographics, purchasing power, city, activity, historical exposures/clicks/payments, lifecycle, and past search-result behavior.
  5. Cross-fit treatment and outcome models. Estimate each action in meaningful units, or explicitly standardize before comparing coefficients. Simultaneous treatments require a joint causal model and residual treatment variation with adequate rank.
  6. If effect modifiers are numerous, a sparse linear final effect model \(\tau(z)=\beta_0+z'\beta\) can summarize heterogeneity. Sparsity and suitable debiasing/inference conditions are additional assumptions.
  7. Use the observational findings to prioritize hypotheses, then randomize promising strategy changes. The source distinguishes observational driver screening from a separate exposure-reversal experiment; a threshold observed in that experiment belongs to the randomized result, not to DML identification. Its specific operating value is omitted from this public teaching edition.

Possible negative associations between content consumption and return-search may suggest attention substitution, but dynamic recommendation and unrecorded interest are alternative explanations. State those limitations rather than turning screening results into proven mechanisms.

Theory and assumptions

Partially linear regression

\[Y=\theta_0T+g_0(X)+U,\qquad E[U\mid X,T]=0,\]

\[T=m_0(X)+V,\qquad E[V\mid X]=0.\]

Define the conditional outcome regression

\[\ell_0(X)=E[Y\mid X]=\theta_0m_0(X)+g_0(X).\]

The nuisance models fitted from \(X\) alone are \(\ell_0\) and \(m_0\), not \(g_0\) and \(m_0\). For binary treatment, \(m_0=e\) is the propensity; for continuous treatment it is a conditional mean.

Subtracting conditional expectations gives \(Y-\ell_0(X)=\theta_0[T-m_0(X)]+U\). Thus

\[\hat\theta=\frac{\sum_i\widetilde T_i\widetilde Y_i}{\sum_i\widetilde T_i^2},\quad \widetilde Y_i=Y_i-\hat\ell_{-k(i)}(X_i),\quad \widetilde T_i=T_i-\hat m_{-k(i)}(X_i).\]

This is a no-intercept least-squares slope. In linear regression it parallels the Frisch–Waugh–Lovell residualization theorem. Cross-fitted residuals need not have sample means exactly zero; the stated moment equation, rather than an automatic centering shortcut, defines this estimator.

Orthogonality

Use the score

\[\psi(W;\theta,\ell,m)=[Y-\ell(X)-\theta(T-m(X))][T-m(X)].\]

At the true nuisance functions, its expectation is zero. Its first derivative with respect to small nuisance perturbations is also zero: conditional mean-zero residuals cancel the first-order terms. Let \(a=\hat\ell-\ell_0\) and \(b=\hat m-m_0\). At \(\theta_0\), the remaining population perturbation includes

\[E[ab-\theta_0b^2].\]

Therefore a convenient sufficient rate condition for this parameterization is

\[\|\hat m-m_0\|_2\bigl(\|\hat\ell-\ell_0\|_2+\|\hat m-m_0\|_2\bigr)=o_P(n^{-1/2}),\]

along with moment, identification, and other regularity conditions. Both nuisance errors being \(o_P(n^{-1/4})\) is sufficient for this remainder. The additional squared treatment-regression error should not be omitted when stating the condition for this particular score.

Here \(o_P(n^{-1/2})\) means multiplying the error by \(\sqrt n\) makes it converge to zero in probability. A root-\(n\) target error shrinks by about tenfold with 100 times the data; an \(n^{-1/4}\) nuisance error shrinks only about 3.16-fold. Orthogonality prevents such nuisance error entering the target entirely at first order.

Cross-fitting and inference

For five folds, train nuisance models on four folds and predict the fifth, rotating until every observation has held-out predictions. Hyperparameter tuning belongs inside the relevant training portion. For repeated users or dependent clusters, split at the appropriate unit.

After solving the moment equation, use an influence-function/sandwich variance appropriate to the sampling structure. With independent observations and \(J=E[\widetilde T^2]\), a basic estimate has form

\[\widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat\theta)=\frac{n^{-1}\sum_i\hat\psi_i^2}{n\hat J^2}.\]

Cross-fitting removes an important overfitting dependence but does not itself guarantee nuisance quality, root-\(n\) inference, or causal identification.

Practice and pitfalls

  • Residualizing only the outcome leaves treatment’s predictable component in the final regression.
  • PLR-DML avoids explicit inverse propensity weights, but near-constant treatment conditional on \(X\) makes residual treatment variance vanish. There is still too little identifying variation.
  • DML is a framework, not an automatic synonym for doubly robust. AIPW-based ATE scores can have either-model-correct robustness; the PLR residual score must be assessed on its own terms.
  • With heterogeneous effects and binary treatment, a constant PLR coefficient generally averages effects with weights related to \(e(X)(1-e(X))\), not automatically the population ATE.
  • Pre-treatment covariates, no omitted confounding, correct temporal ordering, sufficient residual variation, and a suitable structural model remain necessary.

Interview questions

Why two models and two residuals? Both outcome and treatment contain predictable covariate-driven variation. Removing only one side leaves a confounded comparison and nuisance bias.

What does cross-fitting solve? It prevents each record from training the nuisance models used to predict that record, reducing own-observation overfitting bias.

Does DML solve endogeneity? Not unmeasured endogeneity by itself. A PLR analysis relies on the stated conditional mean/exchangeability argument; a valid-IV question needs an IV score or another appropriate design.

What did the return-search analysis establish? It screened potential drivers under observational assumptions. A separate randomized reversal design evaluated key strategy hypotheses and the reported overexposure threshold.

Summary

DML combines an orthogonal score with held-out nuisance predictions. Its precision argument relies on second-order remainder control, and its causal interpretation still depends on the design and estimand.
