Linear Regression: Projection, Interpretation, and Assessment

Learning goals

Explain a linear model, derive OLS as a projection, interpret regression to the mean, and assess fit without confusing training performance with generalization. This chapter covers the regression foundations and assessment material in IDS 11.

Intuition

A regression model describes how an outcome varies with predictors. Hypothesis testing can ask whether a coefficient is zero; regression additionally supplies an estimated magnitude and predictions. The two tools complement each other.

The general linear model is

\[Y_i=\beta_0+\sum_{j=1}^{p}\beta_jX_{ij}+\epsilon_i.\]

“Linear” means linear in coefficients: a model containing \(X^2\) is still linear in its coefficients. Do not confuse the general linear model with the broader generalized linear model family, which includes logistic regression. Indicator variables connect this framework to ANOVA.

Coefficients are attractive because they make a compact model interpretable. Simplicity is useful when it reflects the problem; a simple misspecified model is not automatically scientifically preferable.

Worked example

For \(x=(0,1,2)\) and \(y=(1,2,2)\), \(\bar x=1\) and \(\bar y=5/3\). The slope and intercept are

\[\hat\beta_1=\frac{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2}=\frac12,\qquad \hat\beta_0=\bar y-\hat\beta_1\bar x=\frac76.\]

Predictions are \((7/6,5/3,13/6)\) and residuals are \((-1/6,1/3,-1/6)\). Therefore \(\mathrm{SSE}=1/6\), \(\mathrm{SST}=2/3\), \(R^2=3/4\), and training \(\mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{1/18}\approx0.236\). Residuals sum to zero, and their inner product with \(x\) is zero.

For a separate regression-to-the-mean illustration, suppose \(r=0.42\). A predictor two standard deviations above its mean predicts an outcome only \(0.84\) standard deviations above its mean. This is a conditional prediction under the linear model, not a force making every individual move toward average.

Theory and assumptions

Why squared error?

OLS minimizes \(\|y-X\beta\|_2^2\). Signed residuals alone cancel; absolute error avoids cancellation but has a kink at zero and targets a conditional median. Squared error is differentiable, penalizes large errors strongly, and targets a conditional mean. Other losses produce different optimal fits.

Differentiating gives

\[-2X^\top(y-X\hat\beta)=0,\qquad X^\top X\hat\beta=X^\top y.\]

If columns have full rank, \(\hat\beta=(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y\). Geometrically, \(X\hat\beta\) projects \(y\) onto the column space of \(X\), leaving an orthogonal residual. With rank deficiency, coefficients are not unique, though fitted projections are; use a pseudoinverse or a regularized model. Numerically, QR or SVD is preferable to explicitly forming an inverse.

Which assumptions serve which goal?

Computing the least-squares fit requires no Gaussian errors. Conditional mean correctness, \(E[\epsilon\mid X]=0\), supports coefficient unbiasedness under appropriate sampling. Full rank supports identification. Homoskedastic uncorrelated errors yield the classical variance \(\sigma^2(X^\top X)^{-1}\) and Gauss–Markov efficiency. Normal errors support exact small-sample classical tests; robust or clustered uncertainty estimates address different dependence structures.

A multiple-regression coefficient describes the linear association holding included predictors fixed. It is not a causal effect simply because many variables were included: omitted confounders, post-treatment adjustment, measurement error, and selection still matter.

Assessing predictions

\[R^2=1-\frac{\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}{\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2},\qquad \mathrm{RMSE}=\sqrt{\frac1n\sum_i(y_i-\hat y_i)^2}.\]

The decomposition \(\mathrm{SST}=\mathrm{SSR}+\mathrm{SSE}\) and nonnegative training \(R^2\) hold for unpenalized OLS with an intercept. In simple regression that fit has \(R^2=r_{XY}^2\); in multiple regression it equals \(\operatorname{Corr}(y,\hat y)^2\) when nondegenerate. These identities do not transfer indiscriminately to held-out data, no-intercept fits, or regularized predictions. Test \(R^2\) can be negative, meaning worse squared error than its mean baseline. RMSE retains outcome units; \(R^2\) describes relative improvement.

Practice and pitfalls

Plot residuals against fitted values and time; curvature suggests missing functional structure, a funnel suggests heteroskedasticity, and temporal patterns suggest dependence. Examine influential observations before treating a coefficient as stable. Predicting beyond observed covariate support is extrapolation.

Adding predictors cannot increase the optimized training SSE, but can worsen future error. The curse of dimensionality is particularly severe for flexible local models; it does not mean every parametric regression requires exponentially many observations. Correlated columns make coefficients unstable. With an intercept, encoding every category creates the dummy-variable trap; omit a reference category or use an appropriate constraint.

Interview questions

Why are residuals orthogonal to predictors? This is the first-order condition of the squared-error minimization, not evidence that real-world causes have been eliminated.

Can a high \(R^2\) prove causation? No. Fit is compatible with confounding, reverse causality, and leakage.

Why is a zero training RMSE suspicious? A sufficiently flexible model may interpolate noise. Evaluate predictions on observations excluded from model selection.

Summary

OLS is a projection with a clear objective. Distinguish algebraic properties of the fitted sample, assumptions for inference, and the separate evidence needed for causal claims and generalization.
