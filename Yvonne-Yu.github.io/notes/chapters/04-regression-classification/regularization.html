Regularization and Cross-Validation

Learning goals

Explain the bias–variance tradeoff, derive ridge shrinkage, contrast L1 and L2 geometry, and tune complexity without test-set leakage.

Intuition

A flexible model can chase accidental patterns. Regularization charges for complexity, accepting some bias to reduce sensitivity to the particular training sample. Under squared prediction loss, expected error decomposes into squared bias, variance, and irreducible noise. This decomposition describes repeated training samples, not three numbers observable from one training score.

Ridge spreads weight across correlated predictors. Lasso can make coefficients exactly zero. Elastic net combines both behaviors. Selection of a feature does not establish that it is causal or uniquely important.

Worked example

Take centered one-dimensional data with \(\sum x_i^2=4\) and \(\sum x_iy_i=8\). OLS gives \(\hat\beta=2\). For the objective \(\sum(y_i-x_i\beta)^2+\lambda\beta^2\), ridge with \(\lambda=4\) gives \(\hat\beta=8/(4+4)=1\).

For the lasso objective \(\sum(y_i-x_i\beta)^2+\lambda|\beta|\), the same data give

\[\hat\beta=\frac{\operatorname{sign}(8)(8-\lambda/2)_+}{4}.\]

Thus \(\lambda=4\) gives \(1.5\) and \(\lambda\ge16\) gives zero. The factor of two depends on whether the loss is written as SSE, half-SSE, or average half-SSE. Always state the convention before comparing penalty parameters.

Theory and assumptions

For centered predictors and outcome, ridge solves

\[\min_\beta\|y-X\beta\|_2^2+\lambda\|\beta\|_2^2, \qquad \hat\beta=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y.\]

If \(X=UDV^\top\), its fitted signal uses factors \(d_j^2/(d_j^2+\lambda)\). Directions with little information shrink most, explaining why ridge stabilizes collinearity. As \(\lambda\to\infty\), penalized slopes approach zero. With an unpenalized intercept the prediction approaches the training outcome mean.

Lasso replaces the squared norm by \(\|\beta\|_1\). In two dimensions its constraint region is a diamond, whose corners lie on coordinate axes; a loss contour can touch a corner, producing a zero coefficient. Ridge’s smooth ball generally shrinks rather than selects. Elastic net uses

\[\frac{1}{2n}\|y-X\beta\|_2^2+\lambda\left[\alpha\|\beta\|_1+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\|\beta\|_2^2\right].\]

Here \(\alpha=1\) is lasso and \(\alpha=0\) is ridge under this convention. Strongly correlated features can make lasso selection unstable; adding L2 encourages more stable grouped behavior, without magically identifying the “true” feature.

Practice and pitfalls

  1. Reserve a final test set using a split matching deployment: time, entity, or independent rows.
  2. On the remaining data, cross-validate candidate penalties and feature choices. Fit scaling, imputation, feature selection, and PCA inside each training fold.
  3. Select the penalty using the validation metric, optionally choosing a simpler model within one standard error of the best score.
  4. Refit on development data and evaluate once on the untouched test set.

Standardize features before coefficient penalties so units do not decide which variable is expensive. Keep the intercept unpenalized unless there is a deliberate alternative model. Use nested cross-validation when reporting cross-validation performance after extensive tuning without a separate test set.

Do not call a repeatedly inspected tuning set a test set. Report both training and validation performance to distinguish underfitting from overfitting. Ridge improves conditioning, but neither ridge nor lasso repairs omitted-variable bias automatically.

Interview questions

Why can a biased estimator predict better? Lower sampling variance can more than compensate for additional squared bias.

Does lasso always choose the correct variables? No. Selection depends on signal strength, feature correlations, sample size, and penalty; assess stability.

Why scale inside each fold? A transform learned from validation observations leaks information about their distribution into training.

Summary

Penalty strength is a modeling choice selected with held-out evidence. Ridge stabilizes, lasso sparsifies, and elastic net combines shrinkage and selection. Preserve a final evaluation boundary.
