Overlap, Polarized Scores, and Sensitivity Analysis

Learning goals

  • Distinguish model overfitting from genuine lack of support.
  • Explain trimming, clipping, calibration, stabilized weights, and doubly robust estimation.
  • Interpret Rosenbaum bounds, E-values, and placebo outcomes without treating them as causal proof.

Intuition

A propensity histogram concentrated near zero for controls and one for treated units means comparisons are scarce. Sometimes a model memorized labels; sometimes the business assigned treatment deterministically. These require different responses. Making probabilities less extreme cosmetically does not create missing counterfactual information.

Even excellent balance addresses only recorded variables. Sensitivity analysis asks how strongly an omitted factor would need to affect assignment or outcomes to change a conclusion. It quantifies a vulnerability under a specified model; it does not certify the absence of that factor.

Worked example

Extreme weights and effective information

For a treated observation with score 0.01, ATE weight is 100. Four other observations with weights 1 contribute much less. A common descriptive effective sample size is

\[ESS=\frac{(\sum_iw_i)^2}{\sum_iw_i^2}=\frac{104^2}{10000+4}\approx1.08.\]

Five records may therefore contain little weighted information. Stabilizing a treated weight by multiplying it by \(P(T=1)=0.2\) changes 100 to 20 but multiplies all treated weights by the same constant. Its within-arm ESS is unchanged; stabilization does not fix absent support.

Rosenbaum’s assignment-odds bound

Two matched people with the same observed covariates have treatment odds \(p_i/(1-p_i)\) and \(p_j/(1-p_j)\). A sensitivity model permits

\[\frac1\Gamma\leq\frac{p_i/(1-p_i)}{p_j/(1-p_j)}\leq\Gamma.\]

At \(\Gamma=1\), their assignment odds agree. At \(\Gamma=2\), probabilities 0.5 and \(2/3\) are possible: odds 1 versus 2. For probabilities 0.5 and \(5/6\), the odds ratio is 5.

For illustrative matched outcomes, suppose the worst-case p-value is 0.01 at \(\Gamma=1.5\), 0.04 at 2, and 0.06 at 2.1. The conclusion crosses a 5% significance threshold around 2.1. This says how much hidden assignment bias is allowed in this particular sensitivity model before the statistical conclusion becomes inconclusive. It is not a 2.1-fold treatment effect and does not establish that such bias is implausible.

An E-value calculation

For a risk ratio \(RR=0.08/0.04=2\),

\[E\text{-value}=RR+\sqrt{RR(RR-1)}=2+\sqrt2\approx3.414.\]

This is a risk-ratio sensitivity benchmark for an unmeasured confounder’s associations with treatment and outcome, conditional on measured covariates. It summarizes the minimum equal-strength association benchmark sufficient to explain away the point estimate under the bounding model. Unequal-strength combinations can also explain an estimate; it is too strong to claim that both associations must individually exceed 3.414 in every possible configuration.

For a protective \(RR<1\), use its reciprocal in the basic formula. Also calculate the E-value for the confidence-limit closest to the null; a large point-estimate E-value with a CI touching 1 is weak evidence of precision.

Theory and assumptions

Balance as standardized mean difference

For covariate \(j\), compare matched treatment and control means on a common scale:

\[\operatorname{SMD}_j=\frac{\bar X_{Tj}-\bar X_{Cj}}{\sqrt{(s_{Tj}^2+s_{Cj}^2)/2}}.\]

For a binary covariate, the arm means are proportions and the corresponding variance estimates can use \(p_{gj}(1-p_{gj})\). Keep a chosen pre-match denominator fixed when comparing SMD before and after matching, so an apparent improvement is not produced by changing the scale. Also inspect categorical proportions, variances, and distribution plots: equal means alone do not establish distributional balance. A value such as \(|\mathrm{SMD}|<0.1\) is a diagnostic convention, not proof of ignorability.

A diagnostic sequence for polarized XGBoost scores

  1. Audit timing and leakage. An exposure identifier or post-treatment action may almost encode the label.
  2. Compare out-of-fold with training probabilities. Training-only separation suggests overfitting.
  3. Inspect the assignment policy. Eligibility thresholds may produce genuine zero probabilities.
  4. Check covariate support, not only one score plot. Aggregate histogram overlap can hide empty multivariate strata.
  5. Evaluate balance and weight distributions. Report retained fractions, maximum weights, and ESS by arm.

Shallower trees, stronger leaf penalties, larger minimum leaf information, subsampling, and early stopping can reduce overfitting. Logistic regression or a GAM can be useful baselines. Hyperparameters from a study are starting points rather than transferable guarantees.

What each remedy actually does

Remedy Purpose Limitation
Regularization Reduce fitted-score overfitting Cannot overturn a deterministic policy
Calibration Improve probability calibration on held-out data Cannot restore real overlap
Trimming Restrict to supported covariate regions Changes the target population
Clipping/truncation Limit numerical weight extremes Alters the estimator and introduces bias tradeoffs
Stabilized weighting Control some weight scaling behavior Does not remove within-arm relative extremes
Caliper matching Reject distant matches May lose many treated units
DR/DML/TMLE Reduce particular nuisance-model sensitivity Still require the relevant identification and overlap assumptions

The often-used \([0.05,0.95]\) range is a sensitivity choice, not a universal rule. Report results and population composition under alternative sensible restrictions.

For an AIPW ATE score, with \(\mu_t(X)=E[Y\mid T=t,X]\),

\[\psi_i=\hat\mu_1(X_i)-\hat\mu_0(X_i) +\frac{T_i[Y_i-\hat\mu_1(X_i)]}{\hat e_i} -\frac{(1-T_i)[Y_i-\hat\mu_0(X_i)]}{1-\hat e_i}.\]

Under the causal assumptions, appropriate regularity, and overlap, its mean can be consistent if either the propensity or outcome regressions are correctly specified. This robustness is not permission to omit confounders. The partially linear residual-on-residual DML score has a different robustness structure.

Sensitivity checks answer different questions

Rosenbaum bounds assess departures in treatment-assignment odds within matched designs; implementations include paired and more general matched configurations. E-values use a risk-ratio bounding framework. Risk differences, odds ratios, and hazard ratios cannot all be inserted into the risk-ratio formula unchanged. Translation requires appropriate assumptions and transformations.

A pre-treatment placebo outcome cannot be affected by the later intervention. An estimated “effect” raises concerns about confounding or design errors. A nonsignificant placebo does not prove validity: it may have low power or miss the relevant bias pathway.

Practice and pitfalls

A useful report shows unadjusted and adjusted balance, support exclusions, the retained estimand, absolute effects and CIs, alternative design choices, and sensitivity to unmeasured confounding. Explain which omitted factors are plausible in context—shopping intent, payday timing, private targeting signals—and avoid assigning them numerical strengths without evidence.

Do not turn \(\Gamma\) thresholds into universal rules such as “above 3 is safe to launch.” Sensitivity varies with the outcome, matching design, test, and scientific context. Likewise, a high E-value does not repair selection bias, outcome measurement error, interference, or model misspecification automatically.

Business translation should preserve uncertainty: multiplying ATT by recipient count estimates an aggregate incremental outcome only when the retained target matches that recipient population and the intervention scales without changing effects or costs.

Interview questions

My XGBoost scores are at 0 and 1. What next? I audit leakage and actual assignment rules, compare out-of-fold predictions with a regularized baseline, inspect support and balance, and then consider restriction or redesign. I do not promise calibration or DML will recover missing comparisons.

What does \(\Gamma^*=2.1\) mean? In the chosen sensitivity model, an unmeasured factor allowing roughly 2.1-fold differences in assignment odds can make the result statistically inconclusive under a worst-case allocation.

Why report the E-value for the CI as well? Robustness of a point estimate alone ignores sampling uncertainty. The confidence-limit calculation shows how little hidden confounding may be needed to move the compatible effect range to the null.

Summary

Treat extreme scores as a design diagnostic. State the target changes caused by restriction, and present hidden-confounding sensitivity as a bounded argument with assumptions, rather than a pass/fail certificate.
