Interview Reference and Method Selection

Learning goals

  • Compare tree ensembles by mechanism rather than an unconditional speed ranking.
  • Choose a causal design based on the evidence and estimand.
  • Recall central formulas with their assumptions, units, and failure modes.

Intuition

A formula is useful in an interview when it supports a decision: what to estimate, why the data identify it, how the model learns it, and what could go wrong. Start with the task and data-generating mechanism, then name a method.

This reference brings together the lecture notes’ model matrix, causal-method route, recall list, and further reading. It is a navigation and practice page rather than a substitute for the detailed chapters.

Worked example

A business asks whether to expand a seller support program. Recipients have higher revenue, and a classifier predicts receipt almost perfectly.

First ask whether the target is past recipients’ ATT or the expansion population’s ATU. Audit deterministic eligibility and available pre-treatment histories. If assignment is almost deterministic, changing XGBoost to DML does not produce untreated counterparts. A randomized expansion can identify an assignment effect. A historical rollout may support DiD only with a credible untreated trend. A valid encouragement instrument might identify a complier effect, which answers a different question.

For prediction rather than causality, benchmark regularized linear models and tree ensembles on a suitable holdout. For allocation, evaluate incremental value at the operational budget rather than purchase AUC. The same dataset can support these different tasks, but the claims and validation differ.

Theory and assumptions

Tree-ensemble comparison

Dimension Random Forest GBDT XGBoost LightGBM
Main mechanism Bagging and feature subsampling Sequential gradient-based updates Regularized boosting, often using second-order information Efficient gradient boosting with histogram-based learning
Core intuition Average diverse trees Correct current loss-relevant mistakes Optimize a regularized leaf objective Efficient split search and leaf growth
Typical growth Often deep independent trees Often shallow weak learners Configurable depth/growth policies Commonly leaf-wise, constrained by leaf/depth settings
Optimization information No boosting Taylor step Negative loss gradients Gradients and Hessians Gradients and Hessians for supported objectives
Complexity control Averaging, feature sampling, leaf constraints Shrinkage, tree constraints, subsampling, stopping Leaf penalties, shrinkage, sampling, stopping Leaf/depth constraints, regularization, sampling, stopping
Main caution Correlated trees limit variance reduction Sequential overfitting if unchecked Hyperparameters interact with loss and data Aggressive leaf growth can overfit small samples

Missing-value support, categorical handling, and growth policy depend on the actual implementation and version. “RF requires imputation,” “XGBoost is always level-wise,” and “LightGBM is always fastest” are not universal algorithm facts. GOSS and EFB are characteristic LightGBM techniques, but enabled behavior depends on configuration. Benchmark on the actual data, hardware, and objective.

A causal-method route

Evidence and goal Candidate design Required question
Randomized assignment Difference in means/regression; CUPED for precision What was randomized, and are there spillovers or attrition?
Observational data with baseline covariates Matching, weighting, outcome regression, AIPW/DML Is conditional exchangeability plausible and support adequate?
Pre/post rollout with comparisons DiD/event study Are untreated trends credible and cohorts compared appropriately?
One/few policy units and rich donor history Synthetic control Is pre-fit strong and the untreated relationship stable?
External encouragement or shock IV/2SLS Are relevance, independence, exclusion, and required compliance assumptions plausible?
Treatment allocation by customer characteristics CATE learners/policy learning Is effect identification valid and policy value evaluated independently?
Extreme propensity scores Diagnose/restrict/redesign Is this overfitting or a real absence of comparisons?

Rich covariates do not prove no omitted confounding. Long panels do not prove parallel trends. An externally named variable is not automatically an instrument. Heterogeneous-effect prediction still needs causal identification.

Formula recall: trees and clustering

For class proportions \(p_k\),

\[Gini=1-\sum_kp_k^2,\qquad H=-\sum_kp_k\log_2p_k.\]

For \(B\) exchangeable tree predictions with variance \(\sigma^2\) and common pairwise correlation \(\rho\),

\[\operatorname{Var}(\overline f)=\rho\sigma^2+(1-\rho)\frac{\sigma^2}{B}.\]

The probability a training observation is excluded from one size-\(n\) bootstrap sample is \((1-1/n)^n\to e^{-1}\approx0.368\).

Boosting fits pseudo-residuals \(r_i=-\partial L(y_i,F_i)/\partial F_i\). For a regularized second-order leaf objective with gradient sum \(G_j\) and Hessian sum \(H_j\),

\[w_j^*=-\frac{G_j}{H_j+\lambda}.\]

A candidate split has gain

\[\frac12\left[\frac{G_L^2}{H_L+\lambda}+\frac{G_R^2}{H_R+\lambda}-\frac{(G_L+G_R)^2}{H_L+H_R+\lambda}\right]-\gamma.\]

K-Means minimizes \(\sum_k\sum_{x_i\in C_k}\|x_i-\mu_k\|^2\). Its objective depends on scaling and geometry. Silhouette is

\[s_i=\frac{b_i-a_i}{\max(a_i,b_i)},\]

where \(a_i\) is average within-cluster distance and \(b_i\) the minimum average distance to another cluster. Software needs a convention for singleton clusters or degenerate distances.

Formula recall: causal inference

The balancing result is \(\{Y(0),Y(1)\}\perp T\mid e(X)\) under conditional exchangeability given \(X\) and the appropriate support conditions. An ATE inverse-probability score is

\[\frac{TY}{e(X)}-\frac{(1-T)Y}{1-e(X)}.\]

DiD is \((\overline Y_{T,post}-\overline Y_{T,pre})-(\overline Y_{C,post}-\overline Y_{C,pre})\). Binary-instrument Wald is

\[\frac{E[Y\mid Z=1]-E[Y\mid Z=0]}{E[T\mid Z=1]-E[T\mid Z=0]}.\]

The two-stage representation fits \(\widehat T\) from instruments and controls, then estimates the outcome equation with the projected treatment using proper IV inference.

PLR-DML uses held-out residuals \(\widetilde Y=Y-\hat\ell(X)\) and \(\widetilde T=T-\hat m(X)\):

\[\hat\theta=\frac{\sum_i\widetilde Y_i\widetilde T_i}{\sum_i\widetilde T_i^2}.\]

CUPED uses

\[Y^*=Y-\theta(X-\overline X),\qquad\theta^*=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y,X)}{\operatorname{Var}(X)}.\]

At the population optimum, adjusted outcome variance is \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)(1-\rho^2)\); \(1-\rho^2\) is the retained fraction.

Formula recall: forecasting

AR(p) is \(Y_t=c+\sum_{j=1}^p\phi_jY_{t-j}+\epsilon_t\). ARIMA applies an ARMA model after the selected differencing; MA terms use innovations rather than past outcome averages.

Holt updates

\[\ell_t=\alpha Y_t+(1-\alpha)(\ell_{t-1}+b_{t-1}),\]

\[b_t=\beta^*(\ell_t-\ell_{t-1})+(1-\beta^*)b_{t-1},\qquad\widehat Y_{t+h\mid t}=\ell_t+hb_t.\]

State the seasonal period in observation units and restrict training to information available at the forecast origin.

Practice and pitfalls

Practice each formula in three passes: explain its intuition, derive it from the objective or identification assumptions, then calculate a small example. Finally state one failure mode. For example, the RF variance formula explains why adding trees cannot remove the common-correlation term, and the IV ratio explains why a weak denominator causes unstable estimates.

Keep these distinctions explicit: percentage points versus relative percent; ATE versus ATT/CATE/LATE; observed association versus intervention effect; training error versus future error; confidence intervals versus prediction intervals; outcome AUC versus incremental policy value.

Interview questions

How would you choose a causal method? Define the intervention, population, timing, and estimand; examine assignment and available comparisons; defend the identifying assumption; then select an estimator and inference procedure.

Why not start with the most powerful model? Predictive capacity cannot supply missing counterfactual support or correct an invalid design. Start with a transparent baseline and compare only claims supported by the data.

What makes an answer complete? A concise mechanism, a correct formula or small calculation, assumptions, validation, and a concrete limitation connected to the business decision.

Summary

Use this reference to recover the mechanism and the design argument, not merely the method name. The most useful interview answer connects assumptions, arithmetic, diagnostics, and decision consequences.
