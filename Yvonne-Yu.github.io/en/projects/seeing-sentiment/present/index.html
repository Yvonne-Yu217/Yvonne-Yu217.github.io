Research question

Do news images add predictive information beyond text?

We compare two sentiment channels from the same news corpus, then ask where and when their return associations are strongest.

The central comparison is visual information beyond text, followed by differences across market states and indices.

Seeing Sentiment · Paper presentation 2 / 8

Measurement

Parallel models, different daily aggregation rules

A fine-tuned image classifier and a published Chinese text classifier measure pessimism; each modality has its own daily denominator.

PhotoPes is a share of classified images; TextPes is an average score. Both are sentiment proxies.

Seeing Sentiment · Paper presentation 3 / 8

Measurement validation

Check transfer from Twitter images to financial news

Separate source-domain classification from agreement with language-model labels on unseen financial-news images.

The full news sample shows weak agreement; the stronger-consensus subset excludes about 75% of images.

Seeing Sentiment · Paper presentation 4 / 8

Empirical design

Separate image information from text and market conditions

Joint regressions test incremental prediction; text orthogonalization checks whether the image signal merely reproduces the text signal.

Residual variance is specification-dependent; the residual-return test concerns high-volatility ChiNext observations.

Seeing Sentiment · Paper presentation 5 / 8

Main result

Image-return associations concentrate in high-volatility ChiNext

Compare coefficient ranks with turnover, inverse free-float capitalization, and retail-account share across five indices.

Each retail-proxy rank correlation is 0.80 at N=5, supporting a descriptive retail-participation pattern.

Seeing Sentiment · Paper presentation 6 / 8

Out-of-sample check

Forecast improvement is small and concentrated

Given the sentiment series, expand a 1,512-day OLS training window using PhotoPes lags 3–5; scaling and the historical-mean benchmark use earlier observations.

The 0.293% measures lower squared forecast error relative to the historical mean; it is not a trading return.

Seeing Sentiment · Paper presentation 7 / 8

Stress tests

Control checks support the association; stability is limited

Challenge alternative explanations, split the forecast period, vary the estimator, and separately evaluate the illustrative trading rule.

Regression predictability does not establish stable forecasts or a statistically significant trading advantage.

Seeing Sentiment · Paper presentation 8 / 8

Conclusion

Multimodal predictability has state and market boundaries

The main pattern is concentration in high-volatility, retail-intensive markets; pooled inference is suggestive rather than uniformly strong.

The contribution is to locate where multimodal sentiment predicts returns and how far that evidence generalizes.
