Seeing Sentiment: News Images, Retail Participation, and Chinese A-Share Index Returns

Xiaobin Liu, Kaixin Wang, Yi Yu, Tao Zeng (authors listed alphabetically by surname) Yi Yu is my legal name.

Co-author · Working paper Nov 2024 — Present

We study whether sentiment in financial-news photographs adds predictive information beyond text. The 2014–2026 corpus contains 245,000 articles and 572,000 photographs. Joint regressions and image-on-text orthogonalization compare five Chinese A-share indices. The image signal concentrates in high-volatility ChiNext observations and aligns descriptively with retail participation. Expanding-window forecasts show a small, time-varying improvement over the historical mean. Pooled inference reaches the 10% level; the ChiNext out-of-sample result does not survive five-index correction at 5%.

Research question

Do news images add predictive information beyond text?

We compare two sentiment channels from the same news corpus, then ask where and when their return associations are strongest.

The central comparison is visual information beyond text, followed by differences across market states and indices.

Main result

Image-return associations concentrate in high-volatility ChiNext

Compare coefficient ranks with turnover, inverse free-float capitalization, and retail-account share across five indices.

Each retail-proxy rank correlation is 0.80 at N=5, supporting a descriptive retail-participation pattern.

Conclusion

Multimodal predictability has state and market boundaries

The main pattern is concentration in high-volatility, retail-intensive markets; pooled inference is suggestive rather than uniformly strong.

The contribution is to locate where multimodal sentiment predicts returns and how far that evidence generalizes.

My contribution

I built the news corpus and sentiment measures, ran regression checks, and replicated expanding-window forecasts in statsmodels.
