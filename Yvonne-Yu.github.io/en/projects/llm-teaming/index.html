Three Heads Are Better Than One, If They Share Notes: When and How LLM Teaming Helps Short-horizon Reasoning Tasks

Caijun Wang, Yishu Yang, Yvonne Yu

Research collaborator Feb 2026 — May 2026

We study when heterogeneous language-model teams help short-horizon reasoning and which stage should be shared. Across short-context benchmarks, tested voting, debate, and judging methods underperform direct answering. On ConTRoL, we instead pool sentences from a supplied passage, verify candidate evidence, and delegate the final answer to one model. With Qwen3-8B fixed as answerer, the complete cross-check pipeline, including direct-answer fallback, reaches 71.7% accuracy versus 65.5% for single-model retrieval. Character-level audits check textual support; acceptance and fallback rates describe evidence selection. The results apply to the evaluated tasks and model configurations.

Evidence architecture

Verify shared evidence before one model answers

The verification experiment switches to a Qwen3-8B answerer; retrieval and checking are shared before one of two answering paths runs.

Retained-evidence answering and direct fallback are exclusive routes; both use the original input and the same answerer.

Main comparison

The complete F1 pipeline gains 6.2 percentage points

With Qwen3-8B fixed as answerer, F1 reaches 71.7% accuracy versus 65.5% for single-model retrieval.

The gain compares complete F1, including fallback, with RQA; it does not isolate the verifier’s effect.

Conclusion

Match the collaboration mechanism to the task

The experiments distinguish sharing answers, pooling evidence, and verifying evidence; their results come from different task and model configurations.

The evidence favors testing collaboration at the evidence stage; generalization and deployment efficiency remain open.

My contribution

As a research collaborator, I contributed benchmark evaluation, paired-error analysis, evidence-pipeline design, and validation audits.
